# SOC Role in Blue Team

## Introduction

This room explores the role of the Blue Team in cybersecurity, how security departments are organized within a company, and the career progression of a SOC analyst.

It explains how defensive security teams work together to protect organizations, investigate security incidents, and maintain the security of systems and data.

## Learning Objectives

* Understand the purpose of the Blue Team
* Learn how cybersecurity departments fit into a company structure
* Identify common Blue Team departments and security roles
* Understand the responsibilities of a SOC L1 analyst
* Explore career progression within a SOC
* Learn the differences between an internal SOC and an MSSP

## Task 1: Introduction

This task introduces the Blue Team and its role in defensive security.

The Blue Team focuses on protecting an organization's systems, networks, and data from cyber threats. It monitors suspicious activity, investigates security alerts, and responds to incidents.

The room also introduces the position of the SOC within a company's security structure and explains the skills needed to advance in a cybersecurity career.

## Task 2: Security Hierarchy

Cybersecurity priorities depend on the organization's industry, size, and business needs. Security teams are organized to protect these priorities.

### Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO oversees an organization's cybersecurity strategy and makes important security decisions. This role connects security requirements with business objectives and supervises security departments.

### Red Team

The Red Team focuses on offensive security. Its members simulate attacks and identify vulnerabilities to help improve an organization's defenses.

### GRC Team

The Governance, Risk, and Compliance team manages security policies, assesses risks, and helps ensure compliance with relevant regulations and standards.

### Blue Team

The Blue Team focuses on defensive security. It monitors systems, investigates suspicious activity, responds to incidents, and helps protect the organization from cyber threats.

## Task 3: Meet the Blue Team

The Blue Team can include several departments and specialized roles, depending on the organization's size and requirements.

### Security Operations Center (SOC)

The SOC is responsible for monitoring security alerts and investigating potential threats.

Common SOC roles include:

* SOC L1 Analyst: Performs initial alert triage and escalates complex cases.
* SOC L2 Analyst: Investigates advanced threats and handles more complex incidents.
* SOC Engineer: Configures and maintains security tools, including SIEM and EDR platforms.
* SOC Manager: Coordinates the SOC team and oversees its operations.

### Cyber Incident Response Team (CIRT)

The CIRT, also known as CSIRT or CERT, responds to serious cybersecurity incidents. It supports the organization when incidents require specialized investigation and coordinated response.

Its work may involve digital forensics, malware analysis, threat intelligence, and threat hunting.

### Specialized Defensive Roles

Larger organizations may employ specialists who focus on specific areas of cybersecurity.

Examples include:

* Digital Forensics Analyst: Examines systems, disks, and memory to investigate security incidents.
* Threat Intelligence Analyst: Collects and analyzes information about threat actors and emerging threats.
* Application Security Engineer: Helps identify and prevent vulnerabilities in software.
* AI Researcher: Studies security risks related to artificial intelligence and develops defensive approaches.

## Task 4: Advancing a SOC Career

Starting as a SOC L1 Analyst provides experience in alert investigation, incident handling, and defensive security tools.

A typical career progression includes:

1. Develop core SOC skills and practice through hands-on labs.
2. Participate in cybersecurity challenges and follow security news.
3. Prepare for interviews and apply for entry-level SOC positions.
4. Gain practical experience and explore more advanced roles.

### Internal SOC vs MSSP

An internal SOC protects the systems and data of its own organization.

A Managed Security Services Provider (MSSP) provides security services to multiple customers, often operating SOC services for several organizations.

An internal SOC analyst may work with a smaller set of tools and focus on one organization's environment. An MSSP analyst may handle alerts from many customers, work with different security platforms, and investigate a wider variety of incidents.

### Possible Career Paths

After gaining experience as a SOC L1 Analyst, possible next steps include:

* SOC L2 Analyst
* SOC Engineer
* Incident Responder
* Threat Intelligence Analyst
* Digital Forensics Analyst
* Other specialized cybersecurity roles

Seven security incidents require different specialists. The objective is to assign the correct security roles to each incident based on their responsibilities.

The scenarios cover several areas of cybersecurity:

* Investigating brute-force alerts

* Testing applications for vulnerabilities
* Troubleshooting SIEM availability

This exercise demonstrates why organizations need different security specialists and how teams cooperate to handle incidents effectively.
